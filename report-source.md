# Source ledger: county variation in women's access to mental-health diagnosis and treatment in California

Prepared September 4, 2026. This is the canonical research source used for the user-facing report.

## Scope and assumptions

- Population of interest: adult women in California. Sources limited to birthing people or Medi-Cal beneficiaries are treated as restricted-population evidence, not as estimates for all women.
- Geography requirement: a source must publish a value for a named California county. A county group is not counted as an individual county.
- “Direct access” includes perceived need, unmet need, receipt of counseling/treatment, service utilization, screening, or a diagnosed condition recorded during care. Symptoms, distress, workforce supply, insurance, and overall diagnosed prevalence are proxies unless linked to need or utilization.
- No county estimate has been invented. Counts of represented counties were checked in official raw files where downloads/APIs were available.

## Claim-to-source ledger

1. CDC PLACES 2025 is a modeled county dataset based mainly on 2023 BRFSS; it provides one overall estimate per geography and no sex-stratified estimates. Sources: CDC, “PLACES: Local Data for Better Health, County Data, 2025 release,” https://data.cdc.gov/500-Cities-Places/PLACES-Local-Data-for-Better-Health-County-Data-20/swc5-untb; CDC, “Current Release Notes,” March 16, 2026, https://www.cdc.gov/places/current-release-notes/index.html; CDC, “PLACES Data FAQs,” https://www.cdc.gov/places/faqs/data-faqs/index.html.
2. PLACES depression is lifetime self-report of having been told by a clinician of a depressive disorder; frequent mental distress is 14+ mentally unhealthy days in the past 30 days. Sources: CDC measure definitions, https://www.cdc.gov/places/measure-definitions/health-outcomes.html and https://www.cdc.gov/places/measure-definitions/health-status.html.
3. The 2024 NHIS Sample Adult file includes direct mental-health care and diagnosis variables but public-use geography is not county; restricted geography requires the NCHS RDC. Sources: CDC/NCHS 2024 NHIS documentation, July 21, 2025, https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/nhis/documentation/2024-nhis.html; Sample Adult summary, https://ftp.cdc.gov/pub/Health_Statistics/NCHS/Dataset_Documentation/NHIS/2024/Adult-summary.pdf; restricted geography overview, https://www.cdc.gov/rdc/restricted-nchs-variables/nhis.html.
4. CHIS supports direct county or county-group estimates, with 41 individual counties and the remaining 17 in three groups. Sources: UCLA CHPR, “CHIS Design,” https://healthpolicy.ucla.edu/our-work/california-health-interview-survey-chis/chis-design-and-methods/chis-design; CHIS 2024 Adult Questionnaire, June 26, 2025, https://healthpolicy.ucla.edu/sites/default/files/2025-07/CHIS%202024%20CATI%20v.1.28%2026JUNE2025%20Adult%20Questionnaire_Clean.pdf; AskCHIS FAQ, https://healthpolicy.ucla.edu/our-work/california-health-interview-survey-chis/access-chis-data/chis-frequently-asked-questions-faqs.
5. MIHA county access measures are available for 35 high-birth-volume counties in pooled 2020–2022 data. Sources: CDPH, “MIHA Data Snapshots Dashboard,” https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CFH/DMCAH/MIHA/Pages/Data-Snapshots-Dashboard.aspx; direct CSV, https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CFH/DMCAH/MIHA/CDPH%20Document%20Library/MIHASnapshotsData.csv; CDPH MIHA Methods, https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CFH/DMCAH/MIHA/Pages/Methods.aspx. Raw-file check: all 35 named counties have nonmissing total-population percentages for perceived need, receipt of care when needed, and prenatal/postpartum screening in 2020–2022.
6. CDPH Perinatal Mental Health Conditions at Delivery contains county-residence, hospital-record diagnoses for 2016–2024; county views use overlapping three-year periods and suppress numerators under 11. Sources: dashboard, https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CFH/DMCAH/surveillance/Pages/Perinatal-Mental-Health-Conditions-at-Delivery.aspx; methods PDF, https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CFH/DMCAH/surveillance/CDPH%20Document%20Library/Data-Dashboards/About-the-Data-Perinatal-Mental-Health-Conditions-at-Delivery.pdf; direct CSV, https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CFH/DMCAH/surveillance/CDPH%20Document%20Library/Data-Dashboards/Data-Perinatal-Mental-Health-Conditions-at-Delivery.csv. Raw-file check: 58 county rows; “Any” condition unsuppressed for 54 counties in 2016–2018 and 55 in every later pooled period through 2022–2024.
7. DHCS Behavioral Health Program Performance Data provides county, sex, eligibility, and realized mental-health service use for FY 2019/20–2022/23. Sources: State of California catalog, https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/behavioral-health-program-performance-data; official data resource, https://data.chhs.ca.gov/dataset/380ed3e6-3cef-4aa1-813e-ddad3dd6bb98/resource/4a7fa3df-e3cb-49bc-a56c-dc0a65d5c422/download/adult_utilization_by_sex.csv; DHCS dashboard page, https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/services/mental-health-services-division-default/mhs-performance-dashboard-reports-and-data-mhs-performance-dashboard-reports-and-data/. Official API checks: all 58 county IDs have adult-female rows; 56–57 counties have unsuppressed TOTAL_CT and MHS1_CT components depending on year/delivery system. The utilization totals are unsuppressed for 55–56 counties depending on year/system.

## Reconciled conclusion

No dataset meets all dimensions at once. CHIS is the strongest primary source for the broad original question because it measures need, treatment, barriers, and insurance and can filter current gender, but it provides only 41 counties individually and female-by-county cells may be unstable. DHCS is strongest for all-county realized utilization among adult female Medi-Cal beneficiaries. MIHA is strongest for the pathway from screening to needed treatment among people with a recent live birth.
