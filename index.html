Women's Access to Mental Health Care Across California

Research question: “How does access to mental health diagnosis and treatment vary among women in different counties in California?”

Introduction

Measuring realized service use

This dashboard studies county-level variation in mental-health service use among adult female Medi-Cal beneficiaries in California. The geographic unit is the county, and all 58 counties are represented in the source data.

The data describe treatment use rather than the entire pathway from symptoms to diagnosis. They therefore provide a practical proxy for access, while remaining narrower than the full population of women in California.

Service penetration rate

Eligible adult female Medi-Cal beneficiaries who received at least one mental-health service during the fiscal year, divided by all eligible adult female beneficiaries in the selected delivery system.

Unit: percent (%)

How to interpret the pattern

The map shows geographic variation, but it does not by itself establish statistically significant spatial clusters. Similar shading among neighboring counties may suggest a geographic pattern worth investigating.

The scatterplot describes an association between the size of the eligible population and the observed rate. It does not show that county population size—or location—causes differences in treatment use.

Limitations

Boundaries of the evidence

  • The population is adult women enrolled in Medi-Cal, not all women living in each county. “Female” reflects the administrative sex category available in the source.
  • The rate measures realized service use and is only a proxy for access. It does not directly measure appointment availability, wait times, affordability, unmet need, or whether people who needed care received it.
  • The data identify receipt of at least one mental-health service, but do not separately establish when a diagnosis occurred or distinguish diagnosis access from treatment access.
  • Alpine County's rate is suppressed or unavailable in both systems in every included year. Sierra County's SMHS rate is unavailable in 2019/20 and 2020/21. Missing values are displayed separately and are never treated as zero.
  • NSMHS and SMHS are separate delivery systems whose beneficiary populations can overlap. Their counts should not be added.
  • Claims and encounter records can miss care that was not billed or reported. Changes in eligibility, coding, reporting, or delivery systems can affect comparisons across fiscal years.
  • This descriptive dashboard shows variation and association. It cannot establish that geography or county characteristics caused the observed differences.
